Exercise 123  Qualify the environment and the resource budget

Question
Which distributed backends and physical devices can this workspace actually use?

Technology background

Distributed training on one physical laptop

Distributed training uses several processes that coordinate their work. Each process has a rank, its identifier within a group. World size is the number of participating processes. A rendezvous brings those ranks together. Gloo and NVIDIA’s NCCL are communication backends used in different configurations.

On one laptop, two CPU processes can act as two ranks over loopback and expose coordination, delays or failures. They still share one physical machine. A one-rank NCCL check can exercise the available GPU. Two-device training requires two independently addressable CUDA devices.

Bytes, memory budgets and unified memory

One GiB is 1,073,741,824 bytes. On this 64 GB unified-memory laptop, Windows, the WSL2 guest and a CUDA process describe memory from different positions. The free command reports guest RAM. nvidia-smi reports the management interface’s GPU memory view. PyTorch can query the CUDA runtime for free and total bytes visible to its process.

Keep the name, unit, time and calling process beside each value. The recorded 31,232 MiB management total and 45.97 GiB CUDA total overlap. Adding them would count some of the same capacity twice. Exercise 251 tests what an allocation can actually write and verify.
